Agama Technologies Workspace Documentation

Agama Workspace Accounts, Licenses & SSO

This documentation explains how authentication, personal and business licenses, organizations, roles, and seats work across the Agama platform: ValueSphere, ProcurePath Control Tower, and RevenueForge AI Studio.

It is designed for:

  • Individual users using personal accounts.
  • Business customers using SSO and seat-based licensing.
  • Org admins responsible for user access and SSO.
  • Agama team members onboarding new enterprise tenants.

1. Core terminology & architecture

Agama is a multi-tenant platform with both individual and organization-aware features. These terms appear throughout this documentation:

Personal account

A single user account not tied to a business organization. Best for individual consultants, early adopters, and small experiments.

Internally: the User has licenseTier = "personal" and platform access is stored on the user record.

Business organization

A tenant representing a customer company. Many users belong to the same organization and share a business license and seat pool.

Internally: records live in the Organization and OrganizationMembership collections.

Seat

A seat is an active membership in an organization. Each active member consumes one seat.

Internally: one OrganizationMembership document with status = "active" counts towards the org’s seatLimit.

Platform modules

Agama exposes three main modules:

  • valuesphere — ValueSphere Consulting suite.
  • procurepath — ProcurePath Control Tower.
  • revenueforge — RevenueForge AI Studio.
WorkOS & SSO

All production authentication uses WorkOS AuthKit. WorkOS handles user identities, password resets, and SSO (Okta, Azure AD, etc).

Agama receives trusted identity data from WorkOS and maps it into MongoDB users, organizations, and memberships.

Default organization

A user can belong to many organizations but has a single default organization. This is the context used by the workspace and Settings screens.

Internally: User.defaultOrganization plus JWT payload { uid, orgId }.

2. Personal accounts

Personal accounts are best for solo users or early testing. They do not belong to a business organization, and they are not governed by seats or org-level roles.

2.1 Creating a personal account

  • From the landing page, click Create account or go to /signup.html.
  • You are redirected to WorkOS AuthKit to complete sign-up.
  • On successful sign-up, WorkOS redirects back to Agama and a user record is created in MongoDB.
  • By default, new WorkOS sign-ups are treated as personal:
    • licenseTier = "personal"
    • platformAccess = ["valuesphere"]

2.2 Signing in with a personal account

  • Visit /login.html or click Client workspace in the footer.
  • Click Continue to WorkOS to sign in via WorkOS AuthKit.
  • On success, Agama sets a secure session cookie and redirects you to /workspace.html.

2.3 Personal license behaviour

  • Personal users can adjust their license configuration in the workspace (for example, preparing to upgrade to business features).
  • However, certain products (ProcurePath, RevenueForge) require a business license and may be gated by backend checks.
  • Personal users do not have:
    • Organizations or seats
    • Org roles (owner/admin/member/viewer)
    • Access to the organization admin console

3. Business organizations & licenses

Business customers use organizations to share access across teams. Each organization has a license, a seat pool, and a set of enabled platform modules.

3.1 Organization model

Each organization record contains:

  • name — customer-facing org name (e.g. “BigCo Ltd”).
  • slug — URL-safe identifier used internally.
  • workosOrganizationId — the corresponding WorkOS Organization ID.
  • domains — email domains associated with the customer (e.g. bigco.com).
  • tier — currently "business" for org tenants.
  • platformAccess — e.g. ["valuesphere", "procurepath", "revenueforge"].
  • seatLimit — the maximum active members (seats) allowed.

3.2 Seats and seat limits

  • Each active membership in an organization consumes one seat.
  • Seat usage is computed as the count of OrganizationMembership documents with status = "active" for that organization.
  • If a new member would exceed seatLimit:
    • The membership is created or updated with status = "suspended".
    • The API returns a seat limit exceeded error.
    • The user cannot log in as an active member until a seat is freed or the limit is increased.

3.3 Organization vs. personal licensing

  • For business orgs, licensing is driven by the organization:
    • Org tier (tier = "business")
    • Org-level platformAccess
    • Seat count seatLimit
  • Per-user licenseTier and platformAccess are still tracked but org checks take precedence for business workspaces.
  • Personal accounts are not associated with organizations and ignore org-seat logic.

4. Roles & role-based access control

Agama uses organization-level roles to control who can change settings, manage members, and access the WorkOS Admin Portal.

Role Description
owner Top-level admin for the organization; can manage org settings and members.
admin Operational admin; can manage members and open the WorkOS Admin Portal.
member Standard user; can access products enabled for the organization.
viewer Read-oriented user; can see org details where permitted but not perform admin actions.

4.1 Effective permissions

  • Owners can:
    • Update organization settings (name, seat limit, platform access).
    • Manage members and roles (promote/demote users, except removing the last owner).
    • Open the WorkOS Admin Portal for SSO / directory configuration.
  • Admins can:
    • View and manage members (add, suspend, remove) within seat limits.
    • Open the WorkOS Admin Portal for SSO / directory configuration.
  • Members can:
    • Sign in and use the products granted to their organization.
    • Do not see the organization admin console.
  • Viewers (if used) can:
    • View org details where allowed.
    • Do not have admin capabilities.

Internally, APIs use a helper like requireOrgRole(minRole) to enforce that only users with sufficient role (owner/admin/member/viewer) can access specific endpoints.

5. Vendor guide — onboarding a new business customer

This section is for the Agama team. It describes what needs to happen when a new enterprise customer wants SSO and seat-based licensing.

5.1 Set up the customer in WorkOS

  1. In the WorkOS dashboard, select the Production environment.
  2. Create a new Organization with the customer’s name (e.g. “BigCo Ltd”).
  3. Add their email domain(s) (e.g. bigco.com) to that WorkOS organization.
  4. Configure an SSO connection (Okta, Azure AD, etc.) for this organization:
    • Either use the WorkOS Admin Portal from the dashboard, or
    • Let the customer’s admin use the in-app “Open WorkOS Admin Portal” button (see below).

5.2 First login and org creation in Agama

  1. Ask the customer to choose a designated initial admin (e.g. the lead for this rollout).
  2. Have them visit the Agama login page (/login.html) and sign in via SSO.
  3. On first successful login from that WorkOS organization, Agama will:
    • Create a corresponding Organization record in MongoDB (if one does not exist).
    • Create an OrganizationMembership for that user with role owner.
    • Set this organization as their defaultOrganization.
  4. By default, Agama will initialise the org with:
    • tier = "business"
    • platformAccess including the main modules (ValueSphere, ProcurePath, RevenueForge).
    • seatLimit (e.g. 10) which can be adjusted later.

5.3 Adjusting seat limits and platforms

After the first login and org creation:

  • The Agama team or the org owner can adjust the seat limit and platform access (depending on UI and plan).
  • These settings determine:
    • How many users can be active at once (seatLimit).
    • Which modules (ValueSphere, ProcurePath, RevenueForge) are available to all members.

6. Organization admin guide

This section is for customer-side admins and owners. It explains how to use the in-app admin console and how it relates to your identity provider (Okta, Azure AD, etc).

6.1 Accessing the organization admin console

  • Sign in to Agama and navigate to Settings from the workspace.
  • In Settings, locate the Organization & SSO section.
  • If your role is owner or admin, you will see:
    • Organization name, role, and current seat usage (e.g. 7 / 10 seats used).
    • A “Members” list showing users, roles, and statuses.
    • An Open WorkOS Admin Portal (SSO & Directory) button.

6.2 Configuring SSO via WorkOS Admin Portal

  • From the Organization & SSO card, click Open WorkOS Admin Portal (SSO & Directory).
  • You are redirected to a WorkOS-hosted admin experience scoped to your organization.
  • Your internal IT / security team uses this portal to:
    • Configure SSO integrations (Okta, Azure AD, etc.).
    • Review and update SSO connections in a secure, audited way.
    • Optionally configure directory sync (if enabled by Agama).
  • Once SSO is configured, any user assigned to the Agama app in your IdP can sign in via the standard Agama login page.

6.3 Managing members and seats

Members are created in one of two ways:

  • Automatic on first login:
    • When a user signs in via SSO and WorkOS reports they belong to your WorkOS organization.
    • Agama creates or updates an OrganizationMembership for that user.
    • The membership is activated if there is a free seat; otherwise it is suspended.
  • Admin-driven via membership APIs (and UI where available):
    • Admins can view all members and see their roles and statuses.
    • Admins can suspend or remove members to free seats.

To rotate seats:

  • Identify inactive users in the members list.
  • Change their status to suspended or remove them entirely.
  • Seat usage will drop, allowing new members to be activated on sign-in.

Note: Removing a user from your IdP (e.g. removing them from the Okta group) prevents future logins but does not automatically free the seat in Agama until their membership is suspended or removed.

7. End-user sign-in flows

From the end-user perspective, sign-in is always simple, whether they are personal or business users.

7.1 Personal users

  • Visit /login.html or click Client workspace from the landing page.
  • Click Continue to WorkOS and sign in with your email and password.
  • On success, you are redirected to the Agama workspace.
  • If you forgot your password, use the Reset your password link on the login page. WorkOS handles password reset securely.

7.2 Business users with SSO

  • Visit the same login page (/login.html).
  • If SSO is configured for your organization in WorkOS, you are routed through your IdP (Okta, Azure AD, etc.).
  • Once you authenticate with your corporate identity, Agama:
    • Receives your profile from WorkOS.
    • Associates you with your organization via workosOrganizationId.
    • Activates or reuses your membership, subject to seat limits.
  • If the seat pool is full, you will see a seat-limit error and should contact your org admin.

8. Troubleshooting

8.1 Invalid redirect URI

If you see an Invalid redirect URI page from WorkOS:

  • Ensure the redirect URI configured in WorkOS matches https://www.agamatechnologies.com/api/auth/workos/callback exactly.
  • Verify that the API key and client ID used by the backend belong to the same environment (Production vs. Test) where redirect URIs are configured.

8.2 Seat limit exceeded

If a user sees a seat limit exceeded message:

  • The organization has reached its seatLimit for active members.
  • An org admin should:
    • Suspend or remove inactive members to free seats, or
    • Work with Agama to increase the seat limit for that organization.

8.3 No organization context

If a business user signs in but is not mapped to an organization:

  • Verify that the user is assigned to the correct application in your IdP.
  • Confirm that the WorkOS organization and SSO connection are configured against the right domain(s).
  • Contact Agama support if the behaviour persists; provide the user’s email and your organization name.
